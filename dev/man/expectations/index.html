What to expect

This page is about version 0.4.0, which follows 0.3.0, the first registered release. It says what the release covers, how fast and how accurate it is next to cuDSS, and what it does not do yet. The other pages of the manual have the details.

What the release covers

  • Structures: "SPD", "HPD" (LLᵀ/LLᴴ), "S", "H" (LDLᵀ/LDLᴴ with in-front Bunch–Kaufman 1×1/2×2 pivoting) and "G" (LDU with threshold partial pivoting inside each front).
  • Phases and parameters: the cuDSS phase strings and parameter names through DirectSolver and execute!, the solve sub-phases, iterative and FGMRES refinement, uniform batches, Schur complement mode, matching and scaling, and a LinearAlgebra layer (cholesky, ldlt, lu, ldiv!, \).
  • Types: element types Float32, Float64, ComplexF32, ComplexF64; index types Int32, Int64.
  • Backends: CUDA, AMDGPU, oneAPI, Metal (Float32 only) and the KernelAbstractions CPU backend (Backends).
  • The fused pipeline FusedSolver: one dependency-counter factorization kernel and a partitioned-inverse solve, for real "SPD" and no-pivot "S" matrices with one right-hand side, on CUDA, AMDGPU and oneAPI. Its factorization is bitwise reproducible; deterministic_mode = 1 makes its solve so too.
  • solve_alg = "algo1" on DirectSolver: the partitioned-inverse solve of the fused pipeline after the stock factorization (set before the analysis; real "SPD"/"HPD", "S"/"H" with pivot_type = 'N', one right-hand side, CUDA/AMDGPU/oneAPI). The default stays the level-batched sweeps.
  • KKTSolver: the front end for interior-point KKT systems (refactorization, one right-hand side, inertia), which runs the fused pipeline where the backend allows it and the stock pipeline otherwise or with Bunch–Kaufman pivoting (KKT solver). MadNLP 0.12 binds it as SDSSolver in its own packages (MadNLP).

KernelAbstractions 0.10 (≥ 0.10.3) is required, hence CUDA.jl ≥ 6.5, AMDGPU.jl ≥ 2.10, oneAPI.jl ≥ 2.10 and Metal.jl ≥ 1.11, on Julia 1.13 or later.

Speed against cuDSS

SparseDirectSolver.jl is slower than cuDSS on most matrices, and how much depends on the matrix class. On an NVIDIA H200 against cuDSS 1.0.0 (geometric means of the time ratio, Performance): on the condensed KKT systems of MadNLP the fused pipeline refactorizes at 1.48× and solves at 1.62× the cuDSS time, the stock Cholesky of DirectSolver at 2.44× and 2.83× (solve_alg = "algo1" brings its solve to 1.41×), and LDLᵀ with the default pivoting at 6.0× and 4.2×. On the SuiteSparse and Laplacian matrices, whose wide fronts go through the vendor dense calls, the stock Cholesky refactorization is 9.5× behind (the fused pipeline 1.9×). On the 78k-bus condensed ACOPF system (n = 674 562) the fused pipeline refactorizes in 16.6 ms against 10.4 ms for cuDSS and solves in 2.34 ms against 1.63 ms. The symbolic analysis is faster than cuDSS's on the KKT systems for the Cholesky features (0.40×) and on par for LDLᵀ (1.01×). LU is 13.8× behind on factorization and 15.2× on refactorization (two matrices). The numbers were measured on one GPU architecture (sm_90); other GPUs, AMD in particular, were timed less.

Accuracy

  • Pivoting. The LDLᵀ/LDLᴴ factorization uses Bunch–Kaufman inside each front but does not delay pivots to the parent: a pivot that fails in its front is perturbed to pivot_epsilon (static pivoting, counted in "npivots"). Iterative refinement (ir_n_steps, or ir_mode = "fgmres" after using Krylov) recovers the accuracy; badly scaled KKT systems also need the matching and scaling of matching_alg = "algo5" (Symmetric indefinite systems).
  • No pivoting. The fused pipeline and solve_alg = "algo1" factor "S" matrices in the identity pivot order (pivot_type = 'N'; a pivot below pivot_epsilon is replaced, counted in "npivots"), which relies on the regularization of the interior-point method or on the matching and scaling of matching_alg = "algo5"; KKTSolver (and through it MadNLP's SDSSolver) reports the inertia from the signs of $D$, a replaced pivot counted with the sign it was given. Plain iterative refinement (ir_n_steps, the KKTSolver keyword of the same name) runs on both pipelines and recovers the accuracy that a replaced pivot costs; the fused pipeline has no ir_tol early exit and no FGMRES refinement.
  • "algo1" residuals. The partitioned-inverse solve multiplies by explicit inverses of the diagonal blocks of each front. On ill-conditioned blocks its residual is larger than that of the level sweeps: 1.1–3.3× on the condensed KKT systems, 35–38× on bcsstk38. Iterative refinement removes the difference; whether one step is enough everywhere has not been measured, so "algo1" is not the default.
  • LU without matching fails on hard unsymmetric matrices (rajat21, TSOPF) in both solvers; use matching_alg.

What is not there

Each item raises NotSupportedError or warns once when requested; nothing falls back silently (Parameters).

  • Non-uniform batches (matrices of different patterns in one solver): no API yet.
  • Hybrid host memory and hybrid execution (hybrid_memory_mode, hybrid_device_memory_limit, hybrid_execute_mode): warn once, no effect.
  • Mixed precision, factor_precision other than the input precision: NotSupportedError at the analysis.
  • schedule = "syncfree": NotSupportedError at the analysis.
  • max_lu_nnz: stored and returned by getparam, not enforced (warns once), likewise host_nthreads and use_cuda_register_memory.
  • Bunch–Kaufman pivoting under solve_alg = "algo1", and every other combination outside its scope: NotSupportedError.
  • The fused factorization on DirectSolver (it is reached through FusedSolver only) and CUDA-graph capture of the stock refactorization and solve. Graph replay exists in the fused pipeline only, opt-in through SDSTuning.graph_max_n.
  • Delayed pivots, export and import of the nested-dissection partition tree (user_nd_partition_tree raises), more than one device (device_count).

Backend caveats

  • CPU backend: for development and testing; it is not tuned for speed and refuses the fused pipeline and "algo1".
  • CUDA: the backend measured most; the performance numbers above are from it.
  • AMDGPU: tested in CI on every pull request, timed far less than CUDA.
  • oneAPI: tested in CI on Aurora (Intel Data Center GPU Max 1550). Every oneMKL call synchronizes the host, and the LAPACK steps (potrf, getrf) run on the KernelAbstractions kernels, which are slow on wide fronts.
  • Metal: Float32 only, tested by hand on an M4 Max, no CI. No fused pipeline and no "algo1".

Reporting problems

Please open an issue on GitHub with the backend, the element type, the structure and, if possible, the matrix. The package is developed in agent-driven sessions (see the repository's AGENTS.md); issues the agents open carry the found-by-agent label, and issues from users are welcome next to them.
